# External Systems That Actually Work

Let's look at specific examples of how ADHD founders build external systems to compensate for executive function challenges.

## Example 1: Working Memory - The "Second Brain" System

**The Challenge:**
Maria is building a SaaS product. During a customer call, she gets 5 feature requests, 2 bug reports, and 3 pieces of valuable feedback. By the time the call ends, she remembers... one of them.

**The External System:**

**Tool:** Notion (or Obsidian, Roam, any PKM tool)

**The Process:**
1. **During the call:** Types notes directly into Notion in a "Daily Inbox" page
2. **No organization needed:** Just captures everything in one stream
3. **After the call:** Uses a template that prompts:
   - Feature requests → Product Backlog
   - Bugs → Bug Tracker
   - Insights → Customer Insights
4. **Weekly review:** Processes the inbox (takes 10 minutes)

**Why it works:**
- Zero reliance on working memory
- Captures information at the moment
- Organization happens later when she has energy
- Nothing gets lost

**The Result:** Maria captures 100% of customer insights instead of 20%, leading to better product decisions.

## Example 2: Task Initiation - The "Body Doubling" Technique

**The Challenge:**
James needs to update his startup's legal documents, but every time he sits down to do it, he suddenly finds 17 other things to do instead. The documents sit untouched for 2 months.

**The External System:**

**Tool:** Focusmate (or any coworking app/accountability partner)

**The Process:**
1. **Books a Focusmate session** for tomorrow at 10 AM
2. **Pre-commits** to the task: "I will work on legal docs during this session"
3. **At 10 AM:** Joins video call with stranger who's also working
4. **Social pressure activates:** Knowing someone is watching makes starting easier
5. **Works for 50 minutes:** Even boring tasks become doable with body doubling

**Why it works:**
- Creates external accountability
- Provides artificial urgency (someone is waiting)
- Social context helps ADHD brains engage
- No willpower required—the system forces action

**The Result:** James finishes the legal documents in one session that took 2 months of procrastination.

## Example 3: Planning & Prioritization - The "Forcing Function" Template

**The Challenge:**
Sarah has 30 tasks on her list and everything feels equally important. She bounces between them randomly, making progress on nothing.

**The External System:**

**Tool:** Simple daily template

**The Template:**
```
TODAY'S BIG WIN:
[Only ONE task. The thing that would make today successful.]

SUPPORTING TASKS (max 3):
1. _______________
2. _______________
3. _______________

EVERYTHING ELSE:
[Parking lot for ideas so they don't distract you]
```

**The Process:**
1. **Every morning:** Forces herself to choose ONE big win
2. **Decision rule:** "If I could only do one thing today, what would matter most?"
3. **Works on big win first:** Before email, Slack, or anything else
4. **Supporting tasks only after big win:** Protects the important work

**Why it works:**
- Removes the need to constantly prioritize
- Forces brutal simplification
- Provides clear structure for chaotic brain
- Celebrates wins (important for ADHD motivation)

**The Result:** Sarah goes from scattered progress on 30 things to completing 5-7 important tasks per week.

## Example 4: Organization - The "Zero Maintenance" Capture System

**The Challenge:**
Marcus tries to organize his notes into folders: "Customer Research," "Product Ideas," "Technical Notes." Two weeks later, everything is chaotic again because organizing requires energy he doesn't have.

**The External System:**

**Tool:** Notion with automatic properties and search

**The Setup:**
1. **One database** called "Brain Dump"
2. **Three fields:**
   - Date (auto-filled)
   - Type (Customer/Product/Tech—takes 2 seconds to select)
   - Content (whatever he captured)
3. **No folders, no elaborate organization**
4. **Relies on search and filters** instead of manual organization

**Why it works:**
- Capturing is fast (no friction)
- Organization is automatic (by date and type)
- Search finds everything (no need to remember where you filed it)
- System works even when you have zero executive function

**The Result:** Marcus maintains his notes system for 18 months instead of abandoning it after 2 weeks.

## Example 5: Time Management - The "Buffer Block" Method

**The Challenge:**
Lisa schedules back-to-back meetings: 10 AM customer call, 11 AM investor update, 12 PM team standup. Every meeting runs late, she's always behind, and she has zero time to process information or follow up.

**The External System:**

**The Calendar Rule:**
- Every meeting gets a **50% buffer block** after it
- 30-minute meeting? Block 45 minutes total (30 meeting + 15 buffer)
- 60-minute meeting? Block 90 minutes total (60 meeting + 30 buffer)

**What happens in the buffer:**
- Process notes from meeting
- Do immediate follow-up tasks
- Transition to next context
- Breathe

**Why it works:**
- Accounts for ADHD time blindness
- Prevents cascading lateness
- Creates space for working memory to process
- Reduces anxiety about running behind

**The Result:** Lisa goes from constantly late and stressed to actually having time to think between commitments.

## Example 6: Emotional Regulation - The "Pre-Committed Rules" System

**The Challenge:**
After a customer churns, Tom spirals into "the startup is failing, I'm terrible at this, I should quit." When he gets excited about a new feature idea, he immediately drops everything to build it, derailing his roadmap.

**The External System:**

**Pre-Committed Decision Rules:**
1. **For bad news:** "When one customer churns, I will talk to 3 customers to get perspective before making any decisions."
2. **For exciting ideas:** "When I have a new feature idea, I will add it to the Ideas list and wait 48 hours before building it."
3. **For setbacks:** "When something goes wrong, I will take a 20-minute walk before responding."

**Why it works:**
- Makes decisions when you're calm, not emotional
- Removes in-the-moment judgment calls
- Creates automatic circuit breakers
- Prevents impulsive decisions

**The Result:** Tom stops making reactive decisions and builds more strategically.

## The Pattern Across All Examples

Notice what these systems have in common:

1. **Externalize everything** → Don't rely on internal brain functions
2. **Remove friction** → Make the right action easier than the wrong action
3. **Automate decisions** → Create rules so you don't have to choose in the moment
4. **Design for low-energy states** → System must work when executive functions are offline
5. **Accept your brain** → Don't fight ADHD, build around it

## Your Implementation Challenge

You don't need to implement all these systems at once. That would be overwhelming and you'd abandon them all.

**Instead:**
1. Identify your #1 executive function challenge right now
2. Pick ONE external system from this lesson
3. Implement just that system this week
4. Once it's automatic (2-3 weeks), add another

The goal is building systems that stick, not collecting productivity tools you'll never use.

## Real Talk

These systems feel like "hacks" or "workarounds" at first. You might think "normal founders don't need all this."

That's your inner critic talking.

**Successful ADHD founders embrace these systems.** They don't apologize for needing external support any more than someone with poor eyesight apologizes for wearing glasses.

Your brain works differently. That's not a flaw. Build systems that work with your brain, and you'll outperform founders who rely solely on their internal executive functions.
