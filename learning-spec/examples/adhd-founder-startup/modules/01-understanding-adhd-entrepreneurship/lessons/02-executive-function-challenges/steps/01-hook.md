# The "How Did I Forget That?" Problem

You know the feeling:

**9:00 AM:** "Today I'll finally respond to that important investor email, update the landing page, and schedule customer interviews."

**9:15 AM:** You get distracted by a bug report.

**11:30 AM:** You emerge from a deep hyperfocus session. The bug is fixed! You feel accomplished.

**5:00 PM:** Wait... what was I supposed to do today? *Checks notes.* Oh no. The investor email. The landing page. The interviews. How did I forget everything?

Sound familiar?

## It's Not a Character Flaw

Here's what's really happening: Your brain isn't bad at remembering things because you're lazy or undisciplined.

**Your executive functions—the brain systems responsible for planning, organizing, prioritizing, starting tasks, and holding information in working memory—work differently with ADHD.**

It's like trying to run modern software on hardware that processes things differently. The problem isn't you. It's that you're using strategies designed for neurotypical brains.

## The Hidden Challenge

While the first lesson covered ADHD's superpowers (hyperfocus, creativity, crisis management), this lesson addresses the flip side: **the executive function challenges that make startup work genuinely difficult**.

These include:

- **Working memory** → You open your email app and completely forget why
- **Task initiation** → Staring at important tasks for hours without starting
- **Organization** → Your task list has 50 items and you have no idea where to begin
- **Prioritization** → Everything feels equally urgent (or equally unimportant)
- **Time management** → "This will take 20 minutes" (takes 3 hours)
- **Emotional regulation** → Small setbacks feel catastrophic

## Why This Matters for Founders

Entrepreneurship demands *heavy* use of executive functions:
- Juggling multiple projects simultaneously
- Planning months ahead while executing daily tasks
- Prioritizing between urgent fires and important strategy
- Starting boring-but-critical work (taxes, legal, admin)
- Organizing information from customers, investors, and team members

**Traditional productivity advice doesn't work because it assumes your executive functions work typically. They don't.**

## The Good News

You can build *external systems* that compensate for executive function challenges without requiring you to "get better" at focusing or organizing.

Just like you wouldn't tell someone with poor eyesight to "just try harder to see"—you'd give them glasses—we're going to build external supports for your brain.

Let's learn how.
