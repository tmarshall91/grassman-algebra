# Understanding Numbers and Their Relationships

At its core, number sense is about understanding that numbers represent **quantities** and that these quantities can be expressed in different forms.

## The Number Line: Your Mental Map

Imagine a horizontal line stretching infinitely in both directions. This is the **number line**, and it's one of the most powerful tools for understanding numbers.

```
... -3 -2 -1  0  1  2  3 ...
```

Every point on this line represents a number. Zero sits in the middle, positive numbers extend to the right, and negative numbers to the left.

## Three Ways to Express Quantities

### 1. Integers

**Integers** are whole numbers, both positive and negative, including zero:
- ..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...
- They represent complete units with no fractional parts
- Examples: "I have 5 apples" or "The temperature is -10 degrees"

### 2. Fractions

**Fractions** express parts of a whole using two integers:
- Written as a/b where a is the numerator and b is the denominator
- The denominator tells you how many equal parts the whole is divided into
- The numerator tells you how many of those parts you have
- Examples: ½, ¾, 5/8

Think of a pizza cut into 8 slices. If you eat 3 slices, you've eaten 3/8 of the pizza.

### 3. Decimals

**Decimals** are another way to express parts of a whole:
- They use place value: tenths, hundredths, thousandths, etc.
- The decimal point separates the whole number part from the fractional part
- Examples: 0.5, 0.75, 2.125

## The Key Insight: They're All Connected

Here's what makes number sense powerful: **these are different notations for the same things**.

- ½ = 0.5 = 50% (all represent "one half")
- ¾ = 0.75 = 75% (all represent "three quarters")
- 1/4 = 0.25 = 25% (all represent "one quarter")

When you see these relationships, you can:
- **Convert between forms**: Choose whichever is most convenient
- **Estimate quickly**: "0.48 is close to 0.5, which is ½"
- **Check your work**: "Does this answer make sense?"

## Comparing Numbers

On the number line, numbers to the right are always **greater than** numbers to the left:
- 5 > 2 (5 is to the right of 2)
- -1 > -3 (-1 is to the right of -3)
- 0.5 > 0.25 (0.5 is to the right of 0.25)

This works for any type of number, regardless of how it's written.

## Building Your Intuition

Strong number sense means you can:

1. **Recognize equivalence**: Seeing that 2/4, 1/2, 0.5, and 50% are all the same
2. **Order numbers**: Knowing that 3/5 is larger than 1/2 because 3/5 = 0.6 and 1/2 = 0.5
3. **Estimate**: Approximating 7/12 as "a bit more than half" (since 6/12 = 1/2)
4. **Reason proportionally**: Understanding that doubling the numerator doubles the value

These skills aren't about memorizing rules. They're about seeing the **structure** beneath the symbols.
